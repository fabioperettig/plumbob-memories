It allowed players to create and control their famous Sims in a virtual environment. Originally conceived as a spin-off of the SimCity series, The Sims was an instant success and became one of the best-selling games of all time.
content.about.paragraph2=The enormous success of The Sims marked the birth of a powerful franchise and led to the release of its sequels: The Sims 2 in 2004, The Sims 3 in 2009, and The Sims 4 in 2014. With each generation, its mechanics evolved, making the experience increasingly rich and creative.
content.about.paragraph3=Even so, certain features and quirks make The Sims a unique experience. They belong to the social and technological context of that era and could never be reproduced in quite the same way by its successors. This page is dedicated to everyone who feels nostalgic for The Sims 1, a game that brought about a true paradigm shift in entertainment.
content.willWrightAlt=Portrait of Will Wright, creator of The Sims
content.wright=William Ralph “Will” Wright is an American game designer and co-founder of Maxis. He is known as the creator of three major video game successes: SimCity, The Sims, and Spore. Wright envisioned an entirely new concept in which people could shape stories, actions, and countless possibilities through simulators that were accessible yet powerful — capable of taking the player's imagination wherever it wanted to go.
content.maslow.paragraph1a=Proposed by Abraham Maslow, the theory states that human beings have a hierarchy of needs. We can only focus on higher needs, such as creativity or success, when more basic ones, such as food and safety, are reasonably satisfied.
content.maslow.paragraph1b=In the game, your Sim's Needs panel replicates this hierarchy almost perfectly through eight motives. If you do not take care of the base, the Sim refuses to move up the pyramid.
content.maslow.paragraph2a=Base of the pyramid: These are the biological requirements for human survival: oxygen, water, food, shelter, clothing, warmth, sex, and sleep. If they are not satisfied, the human body cannot function properly.
content.maslow.paragraph2b=If Hunger reaches zero, the Sim dies. If Bladder reaches zero, they urinate on the floor, lowering Hygiene. If Energy reaches zero, they pass out on the floor. While these bars are in the red, the Sim's artificial intelligence enters emergency mode: they cancel the player's commands, wave at the screen, and cry out for help.
content.maslow.paragraph3a=Second level of the pyramid: These are the requirements for stability, order, and protection from environmental dangers. They involve physical safety, guaranteed resources, health, and the search for a predictable environment. Without this stability, the individual lives in a constant state of alert and anxiety.
content.maslow.paragraph3b=In the game, this is reflected in the Comfort and Room meters. A Sim without suitable chairs or who remains standing for too long quickly becomes exhausted. If the house is dirty, full of trash, puddles, or used dishes, the Room meter plummets, ruining the Sim's mood and preventing them from relaxing or doing productive activities.
content.maslow.paragraph4a=Third level of the pyramid: These are the needs for association, affection, and belonging to social groups. They involve the desire to receive and give love, nurture friendships, maintain family bonds, and be accepted. The absence of meaningful connections causes loneliness, isolation, and declining mental health.
content.maslow.paragraph4b=The Social meter determines the Sim's mental well-being. Being isolated for too long causes the meter to reach zero; the Sim becomes deeply depressed, starts crying spontaneously, and waves at the player for help. To restore it, the player must call neighbors, invite friends over, or interact with other Sims.
content.maslow.paragraph5a=Fourth level of the pyramid: These are the needs for recognition, status, respect, and self-esteem. They include the desire for competence and personal independence, as well as the pursuit of reputation, prestige, and appreciation from others. Frustration at this level creates feelings of inferiority and weakness.
content.maslow.paragraph5b=In The Sims 1, esteem operates externally and aesthetically. Keeping the Hygiene meter high prevents immediate social rejection during interactions. Career progression also serves as the ultimate measure of status: to earn promotions and prestige, the game requires the Sim to accumulate a specific number of neighborhood friends.
content.maslow.paragraph6a=Top of the pyramid: This is the need for personal growth, autonomy, and expression of an individual's fullest potential. It involves creativity, the pursuit of knowledge, problem-solving, and self-fulfillment. It is the state in which a person does what they feel they were born to do.
content.maslow.paragraph6b=This is the peak of a Sim's well-being, represented by the Fun meter and the development of Skills. A Sim only reaches the top by using free time to read, play chess, or paint. With all previous needs in the green, the Sim's AI stabilizes, allowing them to study Cooking, Mechanical, or Charisma without interruption.
